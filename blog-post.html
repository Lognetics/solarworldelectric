Solar 101: How a Complete Solar System Actually Works

The five components that turn sunlight into round-the-clock power for your home or business.

By Solar World Editorial· 12 June 2026·8 min read

If you've ever wondered what actually happens between the sun hitting your roof and the lights staying on during a blackout, you're in the right place. A complete solar power system is not a single product — it's five components working together as one. At Solar World Electric Technology, we design, supply and commission all of them as a single, tested system. Here's what each part does.

1. Solar Panels — capturing the sunlight

Solar panels (photovoltaic modules) are the most visible part of the system. They convert sunlight directly into DC electricity. We use Tier-1 monocrystalline panels rated up to 550W and above, chosen for high efficiency, corrosion-resistant frames and strong low-light performance — important for Nigeria's mix of bright sun and overcast, harmattan days.

A quality monocrystalline panel will keep generating useful power for 25–30 years, which is why our panels carry warranties of up to 20 years.

2. The Inverter — making power usable

Your appliances run on AC, but panels and batteries produce DC. The inverter bridges that gap. We deploy Deye hybrid inverters, which intelligently manage three power sources at once — solar, battery and grid — switching between them seamlessly so you never notice an outage. Hybrid inverters are the heart of a modern system and scale from 5kVA homes to 60kVA+ facilities.

3. Battery Storage — power after sunset

Panels only produce while the sun is up. Batteries store the excess so you have power at night and during outages. Your choice of battery determines how long your system lasts:

  • Lithium (LiFePO₄): 10–16 year lifespan, 90%+ usable depth of discharge, silent and maintenance-free — our recommended option.
  • Tubular & SMF: 2–3 year lifespan, a lower-cost entry point.
  • Dry cell: 2–3 years, budget-friendly for smaller systems.

4. MPPT Charge Controller — protecting the system

The Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) charge controller regulates the voltage and current flowing from your panels into the batteries. It squeezes the maximum possible energy out of every ray of sunlight while protecting your batteries from over-charging — directly extending their working life.

5. Mounting Structures — the foundation

Often overlooked, mounting structures secure your panels at the optimal angle for maximum sun exposure. Done properly, they don't damage your roof — in fact, professionally installed panels can shield your roof from rain, sun and debris.

Putting it all together

Individually these are just components. The value is in the integration — sizing each part to your exact load, wiring them safely, and commissioning the system so it performs as one. That's the work our engineers do on every project, with installation typically completed within 48 hours of payment confirmation.
